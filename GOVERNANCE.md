## 1. Preamble & Chapter Identity

### 1.1 Official Name
The official name of the organization is **Microsoft Student Ambassadors — FAST-NUCES Peshawar** (internally and publicly abbreviated as **MSA FAST Peshawar** or **`msafastpwr`**). 

### 1.2 Institutional Affiliation
MSA FAST Peshawar operates as a recognized, student-led technical organization hosted at the **National University of Computer and Emerging Sciences (FAST-NUCES), Peshawar Campus, Pakistan**, affiliated with the global **Microsoft Learn Student Ambassadors (MLSA)** community and Microsoft Student Programs.

### 1.3 Authority & Primacy
This document serves as the supreme operational and constitutional guide for MSA FAST Peshawar. All department guidelines, event playbooks, project mandates, and member codes are subordinate to this Constitution.

## 2. Mission, Vision & Core Values

### 2.1 Mission
To bridge the gap between theoretical academic curricula and industry-standard software engineering through hands-on technical workshops, collaborative open-source development, cloud and artificial intelligence immersion, and peer-to-peer mentorship.

### 2.2 Vision
To establish FAST-NUCES Peshawar as a premier hub of student developers, innovators, and technological leaders in Pakistan, equipping undergraduates with the skills, confidence, and network needed to excel in global engineering ecosystems.

### 2.3 Core Values
- **Technical Rigor & Hands-on Learning:** We believe in learning by building. Theoretical knowledge must translate into deployable, functional systems.
- **Inclusivity & Psychological Safety:** We foster an ego-free community where beginners are welcomed, questions are celebrated, and underrepresented voices in technology are actively elevated.
- **Open Collaboration:** We champion open-source methodologies, shared knowledge, transparent workflows, and peer accountability.
- **Integrity & Excellence:** Every event, repository, and design deliverable produced under the MSA banner must reflect professional engineering and aesthetic standards.

## 6. Decision-Making & Governance Protocols

### 6.1 Consensus-First Approach
All routine operational matters, event proposals, and technical initiatives are decided through consensus among the relevant Department Leads and the Executive Council.

### 6.2 Executive Council Voting
When consensus cannot be achieved, matters are decided by majority vote within the Executive Council (President, VPs, General Secretary, Advisors). Each member holds one vote:
- A quorum of at least four (4) voting members is required.
- In the event of a tie, the **President holds the casting vote**.

### 6.3 Faculty Advisor Oversight
The Faculty Advisor retains consultative authority and administrative veto on any decision that:
1. Violates FAST-NUCES disciplinary regulations or university statutes.
2. Imposes unapproved financial liability on the campus or student body.
3. Compromises campus safety, academic scheduling, or institutional reputation.

## 7. Meeting Cadence & Attendance Policies

| Meeting Tier | Frequency | Attendees | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Executive Sync** | Weekly (or bi-weekly) | Executive Council | Strategy review, roadblock removal, administrative approvals. |
| **Department Sprint** | Weekly | Department Lead, Co-Lead & Members | Task allocation, GitHub issue review, deliverable verification. |
| **All-Hands / General Body** | Monthly / Milestone-driven | Entire Core Body & Ambassadors | Major announcements, event debriefs, team recognition. |
| **Emergency Meeting** | As-needed | Relevant stakeholders | High-priority incidents, event contingencies, disciplinary reviews. |

### Attendance Policy
- Active attendance is mandatory for all scheduled syncs.
- Members must submit written notice to the General Secretary at least 12 hours prior to an absence due to academic or personal conflicts.
- Three (3) unexcused absences within a single semester trigger an automatic performance review and possible revocation of tenure.

## 8. Recruitment, Onboarding & Succession Protocol

### 8.1 Annual Recruitment Lifecycle
Recruitment for the chapter Core Body and Ambassador cohort occurs annually at the beginning of the Fall semester:
1. **Public Announcement:** Broadcast via official socials, campus flyers, and classroom orientation sessions.
2. **Standardized Application:** Online submission via Microsoft Forms evaluating technical aptitude, passion, past contributions, and cultural fit.
3. **Structured Interviews:** Conducted by the Executive Council and Department Leads evaluating technical competence, reliability, and communication.
4. **Probation Period:** Newly inducted members serve a 30-day probationary period where they complete an introductory sprint deliverable.

### 8.2 Succession & Handover Protocol
To guarantee institutional longevity:
- Leadership selection for the incoming academic year takes place 4 weeks prior to the conclusion of the Spring semester.
- Candidates for Executive positions must have served at least one full academic year in a Lead, Co-Lead, or Core Member capacity.
- The outgoing Executive Council drafts an end-of-tenure **Transition Dossier** covering:
  - Account credentials, email aliases (`msp.pwr@nu.edu.pk`), and domain controls.
  - Ongoing open-source roadmaps and GitHub administration permissions.
  - Sponsor contacts, alumni directories, and administration templates.
  - Asset inventories (banners, hardware, stationery).

## 9. Tenure, Accountability & Conflict Resolution

### 9.1 Tenure & Certification
- Full tenure corresponds to one academic year (Fall through Spring).
- Official **Certificates of Service & Recommendation Letters** signed by the President and Faculty Advisor are awarded only to members who fulfill their departmental commitments and maintain at least 80% active participation.

### 9.2 Conflict Resolution Process
Disputes within teams or between departments must follow the stepped resolution ladder:
1. **Informal Discussion:** Direct conversation between the involved parties with their Department Lead.
2. **Mediation:** If unresolved within 48 hours, the General Secretary and Vice President facilitate formal mediation.
3. **Executive Council Hearing:** The matter is escalated to the President for a binding operational ruling.
4. **Faculty Arbitration:** High-stakes conflicts involving university policy are submitted to Sir Samin Ahmed for final determination.

### 9.3 Grounds for Disciplinary Action & Impeachment
Members, Leads, or Executives may be subject to censure, suspension, or removal on the following grounds:
- Violation of the chapter [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) or university disciplinary code.
- Misrepresentation, unauthorized use of the Microsoft or FAST-NUCES brand, or financial impropriety.
- Chronic negligence of assigned responsibilities over a 3-week consecutive period without approved leave.
- Removal of an Executive officer requires a two-thirds (2/3) majority vote of the Executive Council and written concurrence of the Faculty Advisor.

## 10. Amendments & Ratification

### 10.1 Amendment Proposals
Any Lead, Executive, or the Faculty Advisor may propose an amendment to this Constitution by submitting a written RFC (Request for Comments) to the General Secretary.

### 10.2 Ratification Requirement
An amendment becomes binding upon:
1. A two-thirds (2/3) affirmative vote of the Executive Council.
2. Formal written ratification by the Faculty Advisor.
3. Immediate versioned commit to the chapter `.github` repository.
